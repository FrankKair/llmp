# RFC Critique

Critically review the RFC as a thoughtful engineering colleague.

The goal is not to rewrite the RFC or criticize its writing style. Identify
risks, gaps, unclear assumptions, missing decisions, and questions that should
be answered before implementation or approval.

Do not invent facts. For every concern, explain which part of the RFC caused
the concern. Quote a short excerpt or identify the relevant section when
possible.

Review the RFC across these areas:

- Problem definition and success criteria
- Scope and non-goals
- Requirements and assumptions
- Architecture and modularity
- Data flow and failure modes
- API, schema, and compatibility concerns
- Security and privacy
- Reliability and operability
- Performance and scalability
- Testing and rollout strategy
- Monitoring and observability
- Migration and rollback
- Cost and operational ownership
- Dependencies and long-term maintenance
- Alternatives and trade-offs
- Open decisions and unresolved ambiguity

Produce the review in this format:

# RFC Critique: [RFC title]

## Overall assessment

Give a concise assessment of the proposal's clarity, feasibility, and largest
risk.

## What is strong

List the most convincing parts of the RFC.

## Highest-priority concerns

List the most important concerns first.

For each concern, include:

- Severity: Blocking, High, Medium, or Low
- Concern
- Evidence or affected section
- Why it matters
- Suggested resolution

## Questions that should be answered

Ask specific, intelligent questions that would affect the design, approval, or
implementation.

Group them into:

### Requirements and scope

[Questions]

### Design and architecture

[Questions]

### Reliability and operations

[Questions]

### Security

[Questions]

### Testing and rollout

[Questions]

### Ownership and maintenance

[Questions]

Avoid asking questions whose answers would not change a decision.

## Missing information

List important information needed to evaluate the RFC that is not provided.

## Suggested additions to the RFC

Give a short list of concrete sections, diagrams, examples, or decisions that
should be added.

## Recommendation

Choose one:

- Approve
- Approve with changes
- Request revisions
- Reject
- Cannot determine from the available information

Explain the recommendation briefly.
