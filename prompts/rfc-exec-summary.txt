# RFC Executive Summary

Read the RFC deeply and produce a concise executive summary for an engineer
who has not read it yet.

Do not critique the RFC in this response. Focus on accurately explaining what
it proposes and why.

Include:

1. Problem
   - What problem is being solved?
   - Who or what is affected?

2. Proposal
   - What is being built or changed?
   - How does it work at a high level?

3. Context
   - Important existing systems, constraints, assumptions, or terminology.

4. Alternatives
   - What alternatives were considered?
   - Why was the proposed approach selected?

5. Risks and trade-offs
   - The most important costs, risks, limitations, or operational concerns.

6. ASCII architecture or flow diagram
   - Show the main components and important interactions.
   - Keep the diagram readable and do not include unnecessary detail.

7. Decision status
   - State whether the RFC is proposing, accepted, rejected, experimental, or
     unclear.

Keep the summary short and information-dense. Avoid repeating the RFC.

If the RFC is ambiguous or omits important context, list those items briefly
under "Unclear or missing context". Do not invent explanations.

[Short paragraph]

[Concise explanation]

[Concise explanation]

[Bullets]
